Plan

Sell well, spend only where it pays back

The goal is to sell 25 Highland Drive and downsize to a two or three bedroom townhouse or apartment, freeing up equity for an earlier retirement. Every dollar spent on preparation has to come back in the sale price, or it reduces the money you walk away with.

Timing

Spring 2027

List between late August and November 2027, working back from 1 September. If that slips, the fallback is March to April 2028, then spring 2028.

Budget

$50k to $100k ceiling

No fixed budget. Spend is approved item by item. The bigger the item, the stronger the evidence it needs that buyers will pay more for it.

Who does it

DIY first, trades for the hard parts

You do the easy, learnable jobs at your own pace. Trades handle anything complex, licensed (plumbing, electrical, waterproofing) or heavy.

The return test every task has to pass

  • Must items are cheap, fix something a buyer or building inspector would mark down, or are legally required. Do them regardless.
  • Should (worth doing) items are moderate spends that make the photos and inspections noticeably better, compared with similar listings nearby.
  • Could (if agents back it) items are larger spends. They only go ahead if two or more agents independently say buyers in this price range will pay more for them.
  • When in doubt, refresh rather than replace. Cleaning, regrouting, new fittings and paint come before ripping anything out.

How we'll work through it

  • Room by room, in the same order each time: you upload before photos, we talk through the room and your likes and dislikes, Claude writes visions at four levels (Low, Medium, High and Lux), you pick one, and only then do the summary, plan and tasks get written.
  • Agent appraisals happen once the plan is drafted and before any big spend, so the Could (if agents back it) tier gets real local evidence.
  • Visions are written descriptions with costs, plus your inspiration pictures. Claude can't generate restyled images of your rooms from here yet.

Timing rules

  • Garden by April 2027. Planting and lawn work then has autumn and winter to establish before spring photos.
  • Outdoor trade work from April to June. Brisbane's wet season makes it harder from December to March, so the driveway and outdoor tiles wait until then.
  • Quotes from January to March. Good trades book out in the months before spring, so get quotes early and book the autumn work as soon as you've chosen.
  • Bathrooms: refresh before you retile. Lifting old tiles or shower bases can expose leaks, and waterproofing must be done by a licensed trade. Prefer regrouting and new fittings, and only lift tiles with a licensed waterproofer engaged.
  • Heavy work before finishing. Trade work sits in autumn so the garden and finishes are settled before styling in July and photos in August.

52 Goodenia, market to market. It sold for $1.181M in August 2017, a few months after we paid $960,000. By July 2026 it had reached $2.5M, 2.12 times its 2017 price. Ours at the same growth would be about $2.03M. About $190,000 of its rise looks like more than the market, and that's the best guess at what its upgrades added. They refitted the kitchen (new shaker doors, subway tile splashback, rangehood, ovens and a bigger island), renovated the ensuite and replaced the timber floors with light oak. Their ensuite had the same tiled corner spa as ours, and their 2017 kitchen was much like ours today. So getting past $2M would need shutters, a new ensuite and a kitchen refit. This rests on one sale and one automated estimate, and is my reading, not a valuation, so put it to the agents.

In our favour

  • Size. About 345 m² of living space. Only 20 Casuarina Street (340 m²) comes close.
  • Extra rooms. Study, retreat, multipurpose room, rumpus and sun room on top of four bedrooms.
  • Pool and two alfresco areas. 11 of 15 sales at $1.7M or more had a pool, so buyers at our price expect one.
  • Parking. Drive-through garage, a third covered space and side access for a boat or caravan.
  • Three bathrooms. Most sales near our price had two.
  • Recent work. Repaint, roof repair, solar, ducted air and hot water since 2017.
  • Street appeal. The rendered facade, slatted fence and lawn photograph well.

Working against us

  • Clutter. Rooms double as storage, office, music and craft space, which hides the size.
  • Kitchen. Sound, but not updated. 10 of 15 sales at $1.7M or more had a renovated or refreshed kitchen, against 3 of 32 below.
  • Bathrooms. Beige tiles, tiled corner spa hob and framed shower screens. 52 Goodenia ripped out the same corner spa before its $2.5M sale.
  • Window coverings. Dark timber venetians. 10 of 15 sales at $1.7M or more had shutters, against 5 of 32 below.
  • Walls and lights. Pink and beige feature walls and cool downlights.
  • Ceilings. Standard height, which can't be fixed. With more sales read, high ceilings turn up below $1.7M nearly as often as above, so this matters less than it first seemed.
  • Driveway and tiles. Cracked asphalt and mismatched outdoor tiles (tasks 68, 13, 31 and 58).

Cheap fixes that close the gap

  1. Declutter and style every room so the size shows
  2. Swap the venetians for sheers or shutters
  3. Paint over the feature walls and warm up the lights
  4. Renovate the ensuite, the same job 52 Goodenia did
  5. Price a kitchen refit like 52 Goodenia's: doors, splashback and rangehood
  6. Fix the driveway and outdoor tiles

Not every room needs work. 41 Goodenia kept timber venetians and a brown-tiled main bathroom and still made $1.83M.

    Style guide

    Warm modern, sale edition

    Your Pinterest board is consistent: warm walnut timber, charcoal and deep grey, black metal, slatted timber panels, floating shelves, lots of greenery, textured rugs and poufs, and stone-look bathrooms with timber vanities. It's a strong look. For a sale it needs to be dialled back, because dark walls make rooms photograph smaller and narrow the buyer pool. The rule here is light envelope, your look in the layers.

    The envelope stays light

    Walls, ceilings and big surfaces in warm white or a soft greige. Light floors stay as they are. This is what most buyers in this price range expect, and it lets them picture their own furniture.

    Your look goes in the layers

    Walnut-toned timber, black fittings, linen and wool throws, a textured rug, poufs, plants and a few framed black and white prints. All of it moves with you, so it isn't money spent on the house.

    Where your Pinterest ideas fit, and where they don't

    • Yes: matte black tapware and round black mirrors in the bathrooms, warm timber floating shelves with plants, pendant lights in woven or timber shades, linen bedding with a chunky throw, poufs and textured rugs in the living areas.
    • One feature only, if any: a timber slat panel or charcoal wall behind the main bed. It photographs well in one room. Across the house it starts to feel like someone else's taste.
    • Leave for the next home: dark painted walls in living areas, built-in timber feature walls, moody bedrooms and dark stone bathrooms. They cost real money and add risk with buyers.

    Keep and work with

    • Grey modular sofa and white coffee table in the living room
    • Black leather lounges, softened with linen throws and cushions
    • Round black mirrors, lanterns, plants and baskets
    • White storage cubes and drawers, edited down
    • Outdoor furniture, styled as outdoor rooms

    Move out or replace

    • Dark timber venetians in the main rooms
    • Pink and beige feature walls
    • Honey timber bookcases, which clash with walnut and black
    • Bright red, purple and teal rugs
    • Wall posters and flags
