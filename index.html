The plan on one page

Sell well, spend only where it pays back

The goal is to sell 25 Highland Drive and downsize to a two or three bedroom townhouse or apartment, freeing up equity for an earlier retirement. Every dollar spent on preparation has to come back in the sale price, or it reduces the money you walk away with.

Timing

Spring 2027

List between late August and November 2027. If that slips, the fallback is March to April 2028, then spring 2028. The timeline works back from 1 September 2027.

Budget

$50k to $100k ceiling

No fixed budget. Spend is approved item by item. The bigger the item, the stronger the evidence it needs that buyers will pay more for it.

Who does it

DIY first, trades for the hard parts

You do the easy, learnable jobs at your own pace. Trades handle anything complex, licensed (plumbing, electrical, waterproofing) or heavy.

The return test every task has to pass

  • Must do items are cheap, fix something a buyer or building inspector would mark down, or are legally required. Do them regardless.
  • Worth doing items are moderate spends that make the photos and inspections noticeably better, compared with similar listings nearby.
  • If agents back it items are larger spends. They only go ahead if two or more agents independently say buyers in this price range will pay more for them.
  • When in doubt, refresh rather than replace. Cleaning, regrouting, new fittings and paint come before ripping anything out.

How we'll work through it

  • Room by room: you send current photos, Claude gives a costed task list and styling notes for that room, and the tasks land on the dashboard.
  • Agent appraisals happen once the plan is drafted and before any big spend, so the "if agents back it" tier gets real local evidence.
  • Restyled images of your actual rooms aren't possible yet. Your Adobe connector needs reconnecting on claude.ai first. Until then, each room gets a written styling brief and product picks.

A family upgrading into a bigger home. Four bedrooms, a study, several living areas, a pool and two alfresco areas suit a household with school-age or teenage children. They'll want space that looks easy to live in, bedrooms that read as bedrooms, and a backyard that feels ready for summer.

    The mismatched tiles

    Tiles that differ around the house can be handled four ways, from cheapest to dearest. Quotes and photos decide which.

    • Clean and seal so they read as deliberate zones, if the mismatch is mainly colour from wear.
    • Coat or paint with an outdoor tile coating, which unifies colour but needs reapplying and may not suit pool surrounds.
    • Overlay with timber or composite decking, or porcelain pavers on pedestals, over the worst areas.
    • Retile the most visible areas only. This is in the "if agents back it" tier.

    Garden timing

    Do the planting and lawn work by April 2027 so it has autumn and winter to establish before spring photos. Brisbane's wet season makes outdoor trade work harder between December and March, so book driveway and tile work for April to June.

    Style guide

    Warm modern, sale edition

    Your Pinterest board is consistent: warm walnut timber, charcoal and deep grey, black metal, slatted timber panels, floating shelves, lots of greenery, textured rugs and poufs, and stone-look bathrooms with timber vanities. It's a strong look. For a sale it needs to be dialled back, because dark walls make rooms photograph smaller and narrow the buyer pool. The rule here is light envelope, your look in the layers.

    The envelope stays light

    Walls, ceilings and big surfaces in warm white or a soft greige. Light floors stay as they are. This is what most buyers in this price range expect, and it lets them picture their own furniture.

    Your look goes in the layers

    Walnut-toned timber, black fittings, linen and wool throws, a textured rug, poufs, plants and a few framed black and white prints. All of it moves with you, so it isn't money spent on the house.

    Where your Pinterest ideas fit, and where they don't

    • Yes: matte black tapware and round black mirrors in the bathrooms, warm timber floating shelves with plants, pendant lights in woven or timber shades, linen bedding with a chunky throw, poufs and textured rugs in the living areas.
    • One feature only, if any: a timber slat panel or charcoal wall behind the main bed. It photographs well in one room. Across the house it starts to feel like someone else's taste.
    • Leave for the next home: dark painted walls in living areas, built-in timber feature walls, moody bedrooms and dark stone bathrooms. They cost real money and add risk with buyers.
